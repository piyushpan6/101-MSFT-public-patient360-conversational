Sanjay's bar was "a frontier model plus a multi-step reasoning harness, or we get garbage results." 4. What the failures say

  • Tool limits (more than 6 tests on a card, notes over 4,000 characters): the GPT models push harder against the tools' limits than Claude does. The tool tells them, and they retry. Stating the limits in the tool descriptions would likely remove most of these.
  • Missing facts (nodule sizes left out): the clearest quality gap. gpt-6.1-sol didn't show it in this run.
  • Missing data (genomics tables, scans, slides): an artefact of the partial local build. These go away when the rest of the data loads.
  • Speed: every Foundry model is slower than Copilot here, 49 to 69 s vs 36 s per answer. Lower reasoning effort didn't help gpt-5.5 much (57 s at medium vs 69 s at high) and cost little quality.

5. Caveats

  • Not like for like. Copilot runs Claude; Foundry runs GPT. The model, not the harness, is most of the difference. Claude on Foundry is the fair test.
  • Partial data. Research patients, most images, documents in OneLake and the cohort tables aren't loaded, so 34 of 64 cases can't pass for any model yet.
  • One run each, 12 questions. Run-to-run variation is real (we saw two cases swap on the identical Copilot setup). Treat 1–2 case differences as noise.
  • Local mode. Not yet deployed to Fabric; the relay path is written but untested.
